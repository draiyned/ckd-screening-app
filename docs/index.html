Referral

Know when to see a doctor, what to bring, and how to request an appointment.

Go to the nearest emergency room now if you have: chest pain, trouble breathing, confusion, very little or no urine, severe swelling, or vomiting that will not stop.

Ask for a kidney check if

  • Your screening result is moderate or high.
  • Your blood pressure is often 140/90 or higher.
  • You have diabetes or high blood pressure.
  • A close relative has kidney disease or is on dialysis.
  • You notice swelling, foamy urine, or changes in how often you urinate.
  • A lab test shows eGFR below 60, or protein in your urine.

A kidney specialist is called a nephrologist. Your regular doctor can refer you.

What to bring

A short note built from your latest screening and Monitoring log. Show it to your doctor. It is not a diagnosis.

Two kinds of dialysis

      
Hemodialysis

      
A machine filters your blood. Most people go to a center about 3 times a week for a few hours each time.

      
Peritoneal dialysis

      
Fluid is placed in your belly and filters your blood inside your body. It is often done at home after training.

      
Your nephrologist decides which kind fits you.

About Us

    
An AI-assisted screening aid that points people toward early kidney care.

  


  

    
Why this exists

    
Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is often silent until it is advanced. A simple, free check helps people see their risk early and talk to a doctor sooner. This tool does not diagnose CKD. It guides people toward a professional checkup.

  


  

    

      
How your result is made

      
    
        
  • Model score: a Random Forest trained on the UCI Chronic Kidney Disease dataset (400 records). It uses the lab and clinical values you provide.
    • 
        
  • Rule score: a weighted point system for symptoms, history, family history, and lifestyle.
    • 
        
  • Combined: the two scores are averaged into a low, moderate, or high result and a percentage.
    • 
      

    


    

      
Limits you should know

      
    
        
  • This is a student prototype, not a clinically validated tool.
    • 
        
  • The model learned from only 400 patient records.
    • 
        
  • The rule weights are not tested against real patient outcomes.
    • 
        
  • No nephrologist has reviewed the scoring yet.
    • 
        
  • Blood type, last checkup date, and last meal are saved for context. They do not change the score.
